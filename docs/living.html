Living with Dobby

Dobby is not another dashboard. It is the person in the house who knows how the dashboard works.

Every phone and shared screen opens the same household conversation. Say what you want. Use the cards when touching is faster. Open the activity record when you need to know exactly what happened.

Three surfaces. One house.

A morning with Dobby

A morning can look like this.

6:47 AM

  • Maya

    It's cold downstairs.

  • Dobby

    Downstairs is 67°. What should I set it to?

  • Maya

    70.

  • Dobby

    Downstairs thermostat set to 70°.

7:10 AM

A parent opens the kitchen tablet. The same conversation is there. The thermostat card shows what Home Assistant currently reports. It does not pretend that the room reached 70° because Dobby accepted a new setpoint.

That evening

  • Greg

    Set downstairs to 70 every weekday morning at seven.

  • Dobby

    Done. Downstairs will be set to 70° at 7:00 AM on weekdays.

The next morning, the stored schedule fires without asking a model to read the sentence again.

One house. One Dobby.

The first time a browser opens Dobby, it asks who's this? and remembers the answer. That name goes on everything the browser says and does. It is a name, not a login. Nobody gets a permission tier.

The kitchen tablet speaks as whoever set its name last. switch on the plate changes it. Every browser still enters the same persistent thread, with each person's lines interleaved in order.

Dobby would rather ask a stupid question than make a clever guess.

Say what you want. Dobby answers in a sentence or two and stops. This exchange comes from the working rig:

  • Greg

    Set the temp to 74 now.

  • Dobby

    Which thermostat—the main thermostat or the downstairs thermostat?

  • Greg

    Downstairs.

  • Dobby

    Done — downstairs thermostat set to 74.

Four rules show up in ordinary use:

  • An ambiguous device becomes a question. “The thermostat” in a house with two is not permission to set both.
  • An unknown managed device stays unknown. Dobby does not turn “the Roomba” into whichever vacuum looks close enough.
  • A rejected operation comes back with the device's reason. Ask for 85 in a house capped at 78 and the thermostat agent names the limit.
  • An accepted command reports acceptance. It does not report an imagined physical outcome. Home Assistant supplies observed state later.
  • Greg

    Send the Roomba to clean.

  • Dobby

    I don't have a Roomba here—only the thermostats, living room light, and office printer.

The model can still misunderstand a request and choose an allowed action on an allowed device. The closed roster and device rules limit that mistake. They do not make language infallible.

Sometimes talking is easier. Sometimes touching is easier.

Both paths reach the same device machinery. Conversation is a first-class interface, not the only interface.

/house shows every managed device as a card with its reading and its word. The thermostat card has a fader across the range the household allows. While your finger is on it, the proposed number sits beside the fader in amber. The reading changes only when Home Assistant reports a new state.

A thermostat card tap bypasses the model. It is fast, and it still runs the thermostat's range checks. The card never asks “are you sure?” It offers undo · back to 70° after a change. Other device types show their state today. Their controls use the thread or schedules.

The foot of the page holds edit and remove on each card, with add a device below. See Teach Dobby your house.

The eight words

Every state on the board is a word on a flap. There are eight. WARMING and COOLING are one word with two faces.

WordMeans
SetSomebody asked for this value.
Warming CoolingThe device is doing it. Home Assistant said so.
ReadyA schedule waiting for its time.
AwakeA network device that answers.
ListeningDobby is paying attention. On the plate, beside his mark.
QuietA network device that stopped answering.
HeldThe device declined. The reason is beside it.
Not knownNobody has told us yet. Every device reads this for a moment after a restart.

When no word is true, the row has no word. A paused schedule shows none.

The thread remembers what people asked. The activity record remembers what the house did.

When a change counts as something somebody did, a quiet system line appears in the thread: the device, the word, the value, and who did it. That includes a card tap, a schedule firing, and someone turning the dial on the wall.

Then who, in faint ink: Greg for the thread, Greg, card for a tap on /house, the schedule's name when one fired, and changed at the house thermostat when somebody used the dial.

A printer going quiet at night is not a change anyone made. It appears on the card and in Activity, not in the thread.

/admin is a small link at the foot of the cards. It is a room, not a privilege. It holds:

  • Topology. A live diagram of Dobby, the scheduler, every device, and the command paths between them.
  • Health. Whether Home Assistant is connected and whether every runnable schedule has a timer.
  • Schedules. Every standing intention. Pause, resume, delete, or add one here.
  • System. The system settings and the tokens other agents hold.
  • Activity. The full log, newest first. It records tool calls, device changes, schedule firings, refusals, and configuration work.

Tell it once. The house remembers.

A schedule is a standing intention: “Set downstairs to 70 at seven every weekday.” Say it in the thread and Dobby writes a label, a time, the device, the action, the value, and who asked. It says the instruction is set for those times. It does not say the house is already that way.

“By 8 PM” and “at 8 PM” are different requests. Dobby supports the second. It asks when the wording implies the first. At the appointed time, a timer sends the stored command to the device agent. No model runs.

If the model provider is unavailable while Dobby is still running, existing schedules still fire and the thermostat card still works. A stopped Dobby server cannot fire a Dobby-owned schedule.
