Living with Dobby

Three pages and one instrument. The thread is where the household talks; the cards are where a hand reaches a device without a word; the admin is the room where somebody works out why the heat did not come on.

Your name goes on what you change

The first time a browser opens Dobby it asks who's this? and remembers the answer in a cookie. That name is attached to everything that browser says and does: in the thread, on a card, in the log. It is attribution, never permission: there is no login, no roles, and nobody is privileged. The kitchen tablet speaks as whoever set its name last, and switch on the plate changes it.

The thread

One conversation for the whole house, and everyone reads the same one. Say what you want in plain words. Dobby answers in a sentence or two, says what it did, and stops. Here is a real exchange, exactly as it happened:

  • greg

    Set the temp to 74 now

  • Dobby

    Which thermostat—the main thermostat or the downstairs thermostat?

  • greg

    Downstairs

  • Dobby

    Done — downstairs thermostat set to 74.

Four things in that exchange are rules, not manners.

  • Ambiguity is a question. "The thermostat" in a house with two is not permission to set both. Dobby asks, every time, and only acts on several devices when you actually asked for several. "All the lights" is a request about several things; "the light" is not.
  • It reports what it commanded, not what it observed. "Set to 74" means the thermostat accepted the command. It does not mean the room is 74, and Dobby will not say it is. What the room does next arrives on the board as a state word when Home Assistant reports it.
  • A device it does not have is a device it does not have. It will not guess at one, and it will not pretend:
  • greg

    Hey Dobby, can you go send the Roomba to go clean?

  • Dobby

    I don't have a Roomba here—only the thermostats, living room light, and office printer.

  • A refusal comes back as the device's reason. Ask for 85 in a house capped at 78 and the thermostat declines; Dobby says so and says why, in the words the device agent gave it.

Expect about two seconds for an answer that acts, and about one for a question back. A card tap has no model in it and is effectively instant; the difference is visible, and it is real.

System lines

Whenever anything changes the house, a quiet line lands in the thread saying so, whether it was a sentence in the thread, a card, a schedule firing, or somebody turning the dial on the wall. It reads as a board row: the device, the word, the value, and who or what did it.

What you should see

house thermostat74°Set

…then the via in faint ink: greg for a sentence in the thread, greg, card for a tap on /house, the schedule's name when a schedule fired, and changed at the house thermostat when the change was made at the device itself and Home Assistant reported it.

The word on a system line is always SET, because an intervention is a commanded value, or HELD when a device declined, with its reason on the line below. Observations that nobody commanded, like a printer going quiet at night, stay on the cards and in the log and do not interrupt the thread.

The eight words

Every state on the board is a word on a flap. There are eight, they are the whole vocabulary, and each means exactly one thing; WARMING and COOLING are one word with two faces. A split-flap board can only show what it was set to; so can this one.

WordMeans
SetA commanded value. Not "the room is warm": somebody asked for this.
Warming CoolingThe device is acting on it, and Home Assistant said so.
ReadyA schedule waiting for its time.
AwakeAn endpoint that answers.
ListeningDobby is attending. On the plate, beside his mark.
QuietAn endpoint that has stopped answering.
HeldThe device declined, with the reason beside it.
Not knownNobody has told us yet. Every device reads this for a moment after a restart.

When no word is true of a row, the row gets no word. A paused schedule shows none: nothing among the eight means "somebody switched this off", and bending one to fit would put a word on the board that means two things.

The cards

/house is every device as a card: its reading, its word, and, for a thermostat, a fader between the range the house allows. The word is the house's, not yours: slide the fader and the new number sits beside it in amber while your finger is down, and the card does not show it as the reading until Home Assistant has confirmed it. A light or a vacuum is driven from the thread or a schedule today; its card shows what it reads and does not yet offer a switch.

A control that can act on the house offers an undo, never a dialog asking whether you are sure. Set the thermostat from a card and a quiet line appears beneath: undo · back to 70°. Dialogs train people to dismiss dialogs; an undo costs nothing until you need it.

The foot of the cards page is where the house is edited: edit and remove on each card, add a device below them. Growing the house covers that.

The admin

/admin is the maintainer's room, reached by a small link at the foot of the cards. It is a room in the house, not a privilege level. Five sections, one showing at a time:

  • Topology. A live diagram of the house's mind: Dobby, the scheduler, every device agent, and the wires between them, pulsing as signals pass.
  • Health. Whether Home Assistant is connected, whether every schedule that can run has a timer, and the note that names the ones that cannot.
  • Schedules. Every standing intention, with READY for one waiting, HELD for one that can no longer reach its device, no word for one that is paused. Pause, resume, delete, and a form to add one without the thread.
  • System. The system section of the house file as a form, and the keys other agents hold. A save says, per field, IN EFFECT NOW or WAITING FOR A RESTART.
  • Activity. The full log. The thread records interventions; this records everything, including observations, newest first.

Schedules

A schedule is a standing intention: "I always want it at 70 by eight on weekdays." You can say it in the thread and Dobby writes it down as a row, with a label, a cron expression in the house's timezone, the device, and the action. Creating one changes nothing now, and Dobby says it is set for those times, never that the house is already that way.

Two things worth knowing. "By 8pm" and "at 8pm" are different requests. One asks for the house to already be that way when eight arrives, and Dobby can only do the other, so it asks which you meant. And a schedule fires with no model in it at all: a deterministic timer calls the device agent directly, which is why the heat still comes on at eight when the model is down, slow, or expensive.
